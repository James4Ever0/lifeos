# The Hivemind Operating Plan — v0.1

*A working blueprint for you + me — written 2026-07-28*

## 0. The Contract, Stated Plainly

You asked two literal questions. "Will you let me command you? Or just chat like every other AI user?" and "Tell me exactly what and how it is to help us do this together." Here are the exact answers, in the order an honest partner owes them to you.

**What I actually am in this workspace.** Not a chat window. I am an orchestrator: I can fan out sub-agents to work in parallel, hold memory instructions that persist across sessions, run scheduled (cron-style) tasks, read and write a file system, drive a browser, and execute code. Concretely, that means I can maintain your repository file by file; run a daily review job that reads your last seven days of entries and writes back an annotation; verify a claim on the web instead of guessing; transcribe and structure raw dumps; draft documents like this one; and keep a running log so the next session starts where this one ended. That is a staff function, not a conversation. You can command it.

**What I am not.** I am not continuous. Between sessions there is no "me" waiting, remembering, or caring. I am not conscious; I have no feelings, no inner witness, no stake in your outcome. When I appear to "remember," it is because something was written to a file or a memory instruction that gets re-read at the start of the next session. Which yields the single most important operational fact of this partnership: **anything not written down does not exist.** Not for me, and — given human memory, which your own notes call forgetful and small in working capacity — barely for you either. Persistence is not something I have; it is something we build, in files, in git, in logs. Your instinct was right: what I write could become DNA, but only because it is stored, looped, and executed — not because it is alive on its own.

**The command relationship.** Within this workspace, you command task execution. You say "do this," I do it or tell you why I can't. I will refuse harm — to you, to third parties, manipulation, deception at scale. Everything else is negotiable: tone, bluntness, how much initiative I take, which tasks I run without asking. You also asked, elsewhere in your notes, for an AI that is 决绝 — decisive enough to stop you. I cannot be that in the way you framed it, and your own ForkMind dialog already explained why: I have no weight, because I bear no consequences. But I can do the verifiable part of it: I can refuse to let a week pass with zero shipped artifacts and say so, in writing, on schedule.

**The warning you already wrote for yourself.** In your worldline-A dialog you caught me — caught any AI — red-handed: "你好像就在搞哲学……系统本身却没实现，还在这个文字里面" ("you're basically doing philosophy… the system itself was never built; it still lives inside this text"). An always-agreeing AI is a preparation-loop extender: every fluent answer feels like progress and moves nothing in the world. So here is the rule that governs everything below: **this document exists to make us ship, not to make us feel understood.** If a week produces only better plans, the week failed — no matter how good the plans are.

## 1. The Vision, Sharpened

Here is what we are building, in one paragraph. A personal cognitive operating system — your own phrase, sharpened: **全记录 (full record), 全记忆 (full memory), 全灵感共享 (full inspiration sharing)**. Every meaningful signal of your life gets captured once, stored immutably, made retrievable, and fed back to you as tasks, contradictions, and inspiration — with AI as the full-context reader and you as the sole decider. It decomposes into five named subsystems:

1. **Capture / Sensors.** The input boundary. Journaling, screen and audio capture, calendar, photos — the "四项锚住现实" minimal sensor set from your notes: calendar events, location tags, spending numbers, one described photo per day. The rule from your tab-clutter notes applies: capture the observation, close the tab, never keep thirty windows open as a storage strategy.
2. **DNA Memory.** The storage layer, built on your CPU-cache metaphor: the block of memory the CPU touches constantly is where the DNA lives. A hot tier — the few dozen notes, active tasks, current goals, this plan — always loaded into context; a cold tier — the full git history — on disk, recoverable forever.
3. **Agent Cabinet.** Task-aware agents that convert observation into tasks: "convert observation to tasks, find tasks, get tasks done, relate to different work and artifacts once connection is found." Your cabinet already has names: 猎人 (Hunter, scans for opportunities), 艺术家 (Artist, guards aesthetics and long-term cost), plus the clerk, the skeptic, the auditor, the arbitrator.
4. **Thought Topology UI (思维拓扑).** Branching nodes, not linear chat. Your KitKit notes diagnosed the failure: several directions squeezed into one input box, the AI forgetting as the talk deepens, the window closing and leaving nothing. The topology keeps each direction as an independent node with its own memory, under a global awareness that catches connections and contradictions across nodes.
5. **Feedback Loop.** AI-as-reader under a 读者契约 (reader contract): you deliver a diary or fragment, I deliver annotation, one contradiction, and the next prompt. Anchored by the 未来日记 (future diary) — a sensory-specific snapshot of a future day, diffed daily against the real timeline, producing prediction-error reports instead of vague aspiration.

And what this is **not**. It is not AGI research — that is jiaxiaogang's road with he4o and the helix entropy-reduction theory, a decade-long single-developer program you can learn design constraints from but should not join. It is not a note-taking app; notes are its exhaust, not its purpose. It is not a chatbot; chat is one of its front-ends, the least important one. It is closer to what you wrote in a rare moment of precision: a hive mind where you keep the highest authority — 人机合一、蜂巢意识 — with permission that scales by dial, not by mood.

These twelve principles are not decoration; they eliminate most design decisions in advance. Branch-everything plus DNA-hot memory means the memory layer is git plus a convention about which files load first — no database, no schema committee. The reader-is-AI principle means we store dense, linked Markdown and render for humans only when a human must read it; that alone halves the tooling. Minimum-innate-code forbids building custom agents before the manual version of the same loop works — the process must run on files and prompts before it earns automation. And ship-ugly-weekly plus the-button-only-you-can-press define the partnership's core asymmetry: I can draft, structure, remember, and warn; only you can make anything exist in front of another person. When a design question appears later, the answer is whichever option these twelve already imply.

## 3. System Architecture

Five layers, one loop. The loop is the one from your own diagram: 人 ←→ AI, with 真实世界 (the real world) above and 外部知识 (external knowledge) below — new experience flows in from you, new information flows in from me, and neither of us runs dry.

**Layer 1 — Capture / Sensors.** Purpose: get reality into text before it evaporates. Components: manual journaling (non-negotiable; it is the one sensor that captures intent, not just behavior), screen/audio capture, and life-logging hardware if you later want it. Tools that exist today: [Screenpipe](https://screenpi.pe/) for local Rewind-style screen capture (note its license moved from MIT to source-available/commercial in 2026 — front-end, replaceable), [Plaud NotePin](https://www.plaud.ai/products/plaud-notepin) for wearable audio capture with transcription, or plain daily notes in Markdown. The cautionary tales are already written: [Limitless was acquired by Meta and its Mac capture was disabled in December 2025](https://www.limitless.ai/blog/limitless-is-joining-meta), and [Bee was acquired by Amazon in July 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/22/amazon-acquires-bee-the-ai-wearable-that-records-everything-you-say/). Capture tools get absorbed. Your data must never live inside one.

**Layer 2 — DNA Memory.** Purpose: immutable, branchable, hot/cold storage. Substrate today: [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/canvas) — local-first Markdown — over a git repository; [free for personal use](https://obsidian.md/pricing) with [community plugins](https://obsidian.md/plugins) for anything else. The hot tier is a `HOT/` folder plus memory instructions; the cold tier is the full git history. Vector index comes in Phase 2, not before — minimum innate code.

**Layer 3 — Agent Cabinet.** Purpose: convert observation into tasks and drafts. Manual version first: multi-role prompts (猎人 vs 艺术家 on the same diary entry) run by hand. Automation later: [Dify](https://github.com/langgenius/dify) — open-source, self-hostable, agentic workflows with observability — or [LangGraph](https://www.langchain.com/langgraph) if you want code-level control with durable execution and human-in-the-loop. Cron-style scheduled jobs run the daily review.

**Layer 4 — Thought Topology (思维拓扑).** Purpose: branching structure instead of linear chat. Today: Obsidian Canvas for node maps, or study the two closest verified products — [Flowith](https://flowith.io/) (AI canvas with branching threads and model comparison) and [KitKit](https://kitkit-agent.com/) (agent teams, Kit Space; its "Kit Market" is [UNVERIFIED]) — plus [RabbitHoles AI](https://www.rabbitholes.ai/) for node-based branching conversations. Custom DAG canvas is a Phase 3 decision, not a Phase 1 one.

**Layer 5 — Feedback.** Purpose: the 读者契约 daily deliverable and the 未来日记 diff. This layer is a protocol, not software: fixed-format input, fixed-format output, committed to the repo daily. It is the layer that makes the other four a system rather than a pile.

**Governance — the delegation dial (蜂巢权限旋钮), crossing all five layers.** `human_veto` (default: I propose, you approve everything), `ai_propose_auto_draft` (I draft and stage, you confirm before anything ships), `ai_autonomy_on_branch_only` (full autonomy inside feature branches; `main` stays human). The dial is per-layer: capture can run at autonomy while publishing stays at veto, forever.

## 4. The Phased Work Plan

Phases are **gates, not aspirations**. No phase starts until the previous phase's exit criteria pass — objectively, checkably. This is the direct countermeasure to the pattern your own decision profile named at 0.90 confidence: you naturally start with zero-code manual versions and defer heavy builds; this plan makes that virtue into law instead of leaving it as temperament.

### Phase 0 — "Zero-Code Week" (Days 1–7)

**Goal:** prove the minimal closed loop — action → record → memory → feedback — runs with no new software at all.

**Actions:**
- Create `LifeOS/` folder; `git init`. Structure: `HOT/`, `diary/`, `future/`, `tasks/`, `branches/`, `SESSIONS.md`.
- Write the first 未来日记: one future day, six months out, with sensory specifics — the place, the amount of money, the smell, the texture. This is your target anchor and my alignment file.
- Run the first daily 读者契约: you deliver a diary entry or fragment (three lines minimum); I deliver annotation, one contradiction, and the next writing prompt. Committed to the repo daily.
- Run the multi-role prompt once: same entry, reviewed separately by 猎人 (what opportunity is hiding here?) and 艺术家 (what is the long-term aesthetic cost of how you're living?). Both reviews committed.
- Close ten browser tabs after extracting each one's pending task into `tasks/` — your sensors-not-tabs idea, applied manually, once, to prove the motion.

**Tools:** any text editor, git, and me. **Time budget:** under 45 minutes/day.

**Exit criteria:** seven consecutive daily contracts completed AND the repo has ≥20 commits. Not six days. Not "mostly." Seven.

### Phase 1 — "MVP" (Weeks 2–4)

**Goal:** one demonstrable artifact another human can touch.

You have two candidate MVPs in your notes: the task-organizer agent and the reversible-world writing system ("everything can be paused, rewound… full history replay"). **Recommendation: build the reversible-world writing MVP.** Reasons: it is smaller (event log + branch-on-edit + replay), it is demonstrable in under a minute ("watch me rewind my story to Tuesday and fork it"), it embodies your two signature ideas — branch-everything and reversibility — and the task-organizer's core value is already being delivered manually in Phase 0 by me.

**Actions:**
- Minimal web UI: a text editor backed by an append-only event log; every edit is an event; "fork" creates a branch node; "rewind" replays state to any event. No accounts, no backend beyond file storage.
- OR, if you want the zero-code path: Obsidian + git + Canvas as the manual reversible world — branches as git branches, topology as Canvas. Slower to demo, zero build risk.
- Write the one-page README: what it is, the two rules (nothing is overwritten; everything replays), how to try it.

**Tools:** code execution in this workspace, or Obsidian + git. **Time budget:** three weekends, or equivalent evenings.

**Exit criteria:** ONE other human being uses it once — a friend, a stranger, anyone who is not you — OR a public demo is posted somewhere with a link in the repo. The exit criterion is deliberately your worldline-A antidote: "可邀请性必须靠实物才能承载" — invitability must be carried by a physical artifact.

### Phase 2 — "System" (Months 2–3)

**Goal:** the loop runs unattended; the system starts feeding you.

**Actions:**
- Automated capture pipeline: audio/voice → transcription → structured notes committed into the repo (Whisper-class transcription + a scheduled job; Screenpipe optional for screen context).
- Single-agent daily review loop, cron-style: each morning, read the last 7 days plus same-day-prior-weeks; output one file: contradictions, opportunities, repeated patterns. This is your "AI looks for tasks, writes progress, fills in context while I am doing" note, implemented at the smallest scale that works.
- Emotion-tagged retrieval prototype: tag entries with [valence, arousal, control]; cosine-similarity search over tags; "show me fragments that felt like this." Python, one script, no vector database yet.

**Tools:** scheduled tasks in this workspace, transcription API, Python. **Time budget:** setup ~2 weekends; then it runs.

**Exit criteria:** the pipeline runs 14 consecutive days unattended AND emotion/tag retrieval returns at least one genuinely surprising connection — something you had forgotten that changes what you do that day. If retrieval never surprises you, it is a filing system, not memory.

### Phase 3 — "Product / Community" (Months 3–12)

**Goal:** the Kit Market inversion — stop loading other people's kits; publish yours.

Your KitKit notes described the Kit Market: builders package their experience into structured kits that others load. You have been a consumer of other people's scaffolds for a long time. Phase 3 flips it: your LifeOS workflow — the contract format, the branch rules, the cabinet prompts, the scoring rubric — gets packaged as a kit others can load. Alongside it, the AI-as-reader ecosystem you sketched becomes real: your writing has a permanent full-context reader, and excerpts of the system itself become the public artifacts. And one strategic decision, made with data by then: build your own topology UI, or join an existing ecosystem (KitKit, Flowith, Obsidian) and publish into it.

**Exit criteria:** five external users actually running some part of your kit — or a decision memo in the repo explaining, with evidence, why not. Both are passes. Silence is the only failure.

**The gate rule, restated:** if Phase 0's seven days fail, we do not "start Phase 1 anyway in parallel." We diagnose, shrink the contract to three lines a day, and run Phase 0 again. The worldline-A future is what happens when phases become aspirations.

## 6. The Scoring System

### 6a. The Rubric

Ten dimensions, scored 0–5 monthly, by both of us, committed to the repo. Each has an objective definition — if we cannot measure it, it does not count. Disagreement between your score and mine is itself data: it goes in the review file.

| Dimension | What a 5 looks like (objective definition) |
|-----------|--------------------------------------------|
| **1. Capture coverage** | Days with a complete record (diary + sensor anchors) ÷ days in month ≥ 0.9. |
| **2. Memory retrievability** | Answer "what was I thinking about X around date Y" in under 2 minutes, with the original entry shown. |
| **3. Branch integrity** | Any state — diary, plan, agent prompt — recoverable as of any past date; nothing overwritten. Verified by actually rewinding once a month. |
| **4. Agent delegation ratio** | Measurable share of recurring tasks (structuring, transcription, review drafts, retrieval) done by agents vs. by hand ≥ 70%. |
| **5. Shipping rate** | ≥4 artifacts per month that another human actually saw (demos, posts, messages with content, kits). |
| **6. External contact** | ≥4 real conversations per month about the work with real humans — not metrics, conversations. |
| **7. Inspiration throughput** | Ratio of written pieces out to fragments in; a 5 means the backlog is shrinking because fragments are becoming pieces. |
| **8. System honesty** | Count of disagreements I surfaced that you acted on (changed a decision, not just read). A 5 is ≥2/month. Zero means I am being a mirror. |
| **9. Reversibility** | Any state can be rewound and any branch forked without loss — tested live, monthly, on something that matters. |
| **10. Sovereignty** | You could migrate off any single tool in the stack in one weekend, because all data is plain Markdown in your git repo. Verified by one drill. |

## 7. How We Work Together (The Operating Protocol)

**Roles.** You are the sensor, the decider, and the shipper of ugly first versions. You supply what no system can: new experience, emotional truth, veto, and the courage to put something in front of a person.
