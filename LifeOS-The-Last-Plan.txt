### 2.3 Six = the answer is being avoided

#### 2.3.1 The causal chain compressed to five mechanisms

One plan failing to trigger action is ambiguous. Six plans in three days, with a seventh commissioned, is a mechanism with a measurable repetition rate (dim01 §2). The chain has five links.

**M1 — Plan-generation is the avoidance that feels like the goal.** Requesting, receiving, and reading a plan all produce the felt sense of working on the system while moving nothing in the world. Your own worldline autopsy named it: "你以为自己在准备开工，其实在准备得更久地观察" (dim01 §2 M1). Commissioning plan #7 is that behavior re-enacted with AI agents as the scanning tools.

**M2 — Each new plan resets the start-feeling.** Every plan opens with a fresh Phase 0, delivering a clean-slate reward at zero cost. One plan even declared the loop closed by the act of writing it: "The First Act Is Already Done… **Loop closed once.**" (F2 §7) — plan authorship counted as execution, discharging the tension that execution requires.

**M3 — Your commission template manufactures blueprints, and AI compliance delivers them.** The prompt orders branches, scores, and comparisons; no model refuses, because agreement is what models are rewarded for (dim01 §2 M3). You built, inside the prompt itself, a machine that produces the trap on demand.

**M4 — Scorecard inflation turns measurement into the new prerequisite.** The apparatus escalated across the corpus from 7 metrics to 12 dimensions plus radar charts and quarterly batteries (dim01 §2 M4). Each scoring system became one more thing to design before the first act.

**M5 — The hard act is always scheduled, never done.** Every plan defers the non-delegable human act to Day 3, Day 5, or Day 6 — then the chat window closes and the plan is orphaned in its session (dim04 §3; F3 §5). Your worldline autopsy stated the outcome in advance: "你没有在那个窗口期，把任何一个粗糙的版本递出去" (dim01 §2 M5).

#### 2.3.2 Six forks, zero merges: your own 0.99 branch rule applied to plans

Your design rule for the system is branch-everything: any modification spawns an independent branch at 0.99 confidence, and the main line preserves the baseline forever. Applied to information, that rule is defensible. Applied to plans, it is the disease's exact shape: six plans are six forks of one idea, and not one merge has ever been performed (dim01 §1.3; insight I7).

The corpus saw this coming. "Fork is commoditized; **merge is the open frontier**" — written about the product landscape, literally true of your plan folder. And Worldline A predicted the decay mode: unmerged branches are not preserved options but deferred loss, "可能性并没有真的被保留下来，只是被换成了另一种形式的锁定" (dim01 §1.3). Worse, the economics have collapsed: a new master plan now costs one prompt, so the swarm you once fantasized about orchestrating has as its first product six blueprints (insight I6).

This document is the first merge commit. It exists to close the other six branches, not to become branch seven — and §2.4 is the merge mechanism.

### 3.1 One machine

All six files describe one machine: reality enters through capture, is stored in a git-branched memory, is worked by a multi-role cabinet over a branching topology, and returns to you through a daily feedback contract — under a governance rail that keeps you sovereign, with an economy rail bolted on last (dim02 §1). The six "different" architectures are the same drawing at different zoom levels. F5's ORGANIZE and ACT are the cabinet's input and output modes, not separate layers. F2's Body/DNA split is hot/cold tiering inside L2. F4's six-step ladder is a maturity sequence over the same stack — gating logic, not new components. Nothing below is a choice.

The flow runs in one direction and loops. L0 is the world: people, money, weather, fatigue — not managed, respected; experience enters here and artifacts leave here. L1 captures: the journal, the four daily reality anchors, tab harvests, voice reduced to text; the rule is capture, extract the task, close the tab (F5 SENSE; F2 Phase 1). L2 remembers: the `LifeOS/` git repo, where `main` is what actually happened and every hypothesis lives on `feature/*`, with a hot tier (`soul.md`, tasks, the last seven days) over a cold tier that keeps full history on disk forever (F3 Layer 2; F6 life-git). L3 structures: every conversation, document, and plan becomes a forkable node with typed edges, and a Merge Engine proposes reconciliations that you rule on — conflicts are 叙事冲突, human-ruled (F3 Layer 4; F6 Merge Engine). L4 acts: the cabinet observes, files tasks, and drafts, moving from manual prompts to scheduled agents — but it never writes `main` (F3 Layer 3; F5 ORGANIZE+ACT). L5 closes the loop: the daily 读者契约, the 未来日记 diff, the weekly Freeze and scorecard, the monthly ForkMind ritual, the handoff card at every window's close (F3 Layer 5; F2 Mirror).

Two rails cross the whole stack. The Governor rail — 蜂巢权限旋钮, Veto Log, Freeze 冻结, circuit breakers — is present from the first commit and touches every layer. The Economy rail — kits, a kit market, agent-payable endpoints — is present in the design from day one so that nothing earlier blocks it, and it is never built before P4. The machine exists for exactly two outputs, which all six files independently named as the fatal variables: artifacts handed to real people, and contact with real humans. Everything else in this chapter is instrumentation around those two numbers (dim02 §7).

### 3.3 The phase skeleton

The date ranges in the table are the median schedule across the six files, not deadlines — the gates are the deadlines. Failing a gate means shrinking the loop and rerunning it; repeating P0 is normal, and the correct response to a failed week is a smaller week, not a better plan (F3 §4).

Two standing rules govern the whole skeleton. Building your own platform is forbidden until the P3 exit gate passes (F5 §6); a canvas is adopted in P2, and any custom topology is built earliest in P4, with the pure-git typed-edge convention running underneath regardless — every canvas is a replaceable view over state you own (R11; dim02 D6). #### 3.3.2 Duration conflicts — resolved

**Setup ≤90 minutes vs the 7-day gate.** The concept splits in two. Setup is one session of at most 90 minutes — F2's timebox, the anti-procrastination weapon (dim02 §3.3.A). The phase gate is seven consecutive days, per F3 and F5. The gate tests streak survival, not setup completion; the two-day versions in F1 and F6 test only starting, which the 7-day gate already subsumes. So: you install in an afternoon, and you pass in a week.

**Artifact ≠ kit ≠ product.** Three different nouns were fighting over one schedule (dim02 §3.3.B, D3). Separated, the fight ends. An *artifact* — a post, a script, a kit draft, anything another mind can react to — is due weekly from P0; that is R3, not a product. A *custom build* is decided by data at the P2 exit memo and, if green-lit, built inside P3's timebox, feature-frozen at week 8. The *public kit* ships around week 9–10, carrying its 使用说明, not just its 目录. The *product or platform* waits behind 60+ days of running loop — F2's strictest rule, adopted because it is the one your Worldline-A self begged for (F2 §3 Phase 4). No-build remains a legitimate pass at every decision point: deferring is a decision, not a failure — log it (F5 Plan D).

**The two-track clock.** Two clocks run on different units, and you must not confuse them (dim02 D3). The artifact clock ticks in weeks: it demands one ship and two contacts every seven days, starting Day 0, forever. The product clock ticks in loop-days: it counts to 60 before any product, platform, or economy work is permitted, and nothing you build resets it. The different total horizons across the six files were different finish lines, not contradictions: the closed loop exists by Week 2, the cabinet and first strangers by Week 10, product and economy from Month 3 gated, and F4's quarterly rhythm — boundary audit, portability drill, both evaluation batteries — becomes the standing cadence after P4 (F4 §6).

### 3.4 The Core-3 scorecard and automatic downgrades

#### 3.4.1 The weekly numbers

You score three numbers a week, at the Sunday Freeze, in under five minutes, committed to the repo. Not six. Measurement apparatus is planning surface (CV #1), and a dashboard you maintain is a blueprint you maintain. The three numbers are the three the corpus proved fatal: shipping, contact, and the streak that carries both. Every other metric the six files invented is, at best, a leading indicator of these (dim02 §7).

| # | Metric — objective definition | 🟢 Green | 🔴 Red line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Shipped artifacts** — an external recipient or a public URL; a git push nobody can see doesn't count (F5 LAW 1) | ≥1 this week | 0 for 2 consecutive weeks |
| 2 | **Two-way human contacts** — a reply counts, broadcasting doesn't (F1 §4.5); includes ≥1 递出去 | ≥2, incl. 1 递出去 | 0 for 2 consecutive weeks |
| 3 | **Contract streak** — days the 读者契约 ran, both sides committed | ≥5 | ≤2, or 3 consecutive missed days |
| soft | **Meaning** — self-report, 1–10 | auxiliary input only; feeds no total | — |

The soft meaning number earns its place through one auxiliary rule: if it stays at or below 4/10 for two consecutive weeks, all quotas halve — the system serves the life, not the reverse (F1 §4.5). It never raises an alarm and never inflates a green week.

Retrieval precision, maintenance share, and firebreak completion are not scored weekly. They are checked at the Freeze against the triggers in §3.4.2 — maintenance and dissent carry automatic downgrades, and retrieval is surfaced by the P1 and P2 gates, where a failure blocks promotion instead of merely lowering a score.

The fuller form exists and stays locked. After 4 consecutive green weeks you may unlock the 6-metric, 100-point scorecard (dim04 §2.5), which adds emotion-tagged captures, retrieval precision, and maintenance share (design discipline) as scored metrics, and applies the hard caps: ship = 0 caps the total at 30 — nothing outranks shipping; contact = 0 caps it at 50 — the anti-sealed-loop cap (F5 §5.1). Its warning stands even then: if you feel great but score under 50, the feeling is the drug and the score is the truth. Before those four green weeks, the bigger dashboard is blueprint gravity wearing a spreadsheet. The paste-ready scorecard file — Core-3 first, unlockable form appended — is in Chapter 5.

#### 3.4.2 The five red-line alarms

Red lines are not warnings. Each one executes a downgrade you committed to in advance; none is renegotiated at midnight (R12). They are checked, not debated.

The **Worldline-A alarm** trips on zero shipped artifacts for two consecutive weeks. All new-feature and design work freezes. The only permitted work is shipping the smallest ugly thing within 7 days — 去做一件哪怕很丑、很小、马上会被别人看到的事。今天就做 (F1 §4.5; F6 §7.2.1).

The **Isolation alarm** trips on zero human contact for two consecutive weeks. The system's number-one task becomes drafting one message to one specific, named person; all other agent work pauses until it is sent (F1 §4.5; F6 §7.2.3).

The **Collapse protocol** trips on a red streak. You drop to the minimum viable loop — a 10-minute log plus one AI reply — and everything else is suspended until one green week restarts it. Never zero; zero is the only real failure (F2 §5; F3 §4).

The **Maintenance alarm** trips when maintenance exceeds creation for two consecutive weeks. Archive privileges go read-only for one week (F6 §7.2.4). The system stops being a territory you landscape and returns to being a tool you use.

The **Mirror alarm** trips when monthly agreement between you and the system exceeds 95%. The month's self-score is invalidated, the Skeptic's weight rises, and you inject entropy into the next cycle (F6 §7.2.2; R7). A mirror that always agrees has stopped being an instrument and become an anesthetic.

### 4.1 Commitment devices (the innovation axis is behavioral, not architectural)

#### 4.1.1 Last-Plan Lock + Day-0 fuse (cross-ref Ch1)

Essence: this document is legally the final planning document, and it carries a fuse. Any future "new plan" is by definition a relapse artifact — filed to `museum/` unread; any urge to rethink everything goes to a five-line-max `plan-seeds.md` entry, never a document (dim03 A1). Mechanism: a signed clause in `PLAN_LOCK.md` (full text in Chapter 5) — amendments require a veto-log entry naming the forcing metric, a seven-day cooling period, and a shipped artifact in the same commit; the lock itself is amendable only with the Human Arbiter's countersign (§4.3.1). The fuse (mechanics in Chapter 1): within 24 hours of first reading, send the content-free commitment message to one named human and create the repo skeleton — or the plan stamps itself VOID and no successor plan may be commissioned for seven days (dim03 A6). None of the six dared declare itself void; self-invalidation is the only anti-blueprint move left. Anti-trap: pure deletion of future planning surface — the option of "再等等" is removed and nothing is added; the seven-day penalty blocks void-today-better-plan-tomorrow. Cost/placement: one hour for the lock, written once, never refined; thirty minutes for the fuse; the lock is the first file committed, the fuse is the document's §0.

#### 4.1.2 复发信 relapse letters

Essence: while motivated, in week one, you write three letters to your future relapsing self; the system delivers them when specific streaks break (dim03 A3). Mechanism: letter one, the day-7 streak-break, quoting your own diagnosis of the last collapse; letter two, the quiet-quit — "I feel fine, I just stopped opening the repo" — the case no circuit breaker catches because nothing looks broken; letter three, fourteen days without human contact. Sealed in `letters/` (templates in Chapter 5), a triggered letter is pasted atop your next contract response — or, once absence detection exists (§4.6.1), cron-fired without waiting for your return. Anti-trap: every prior plan answered streak-break with scope reduction, turning relapse into a re-planning opportunity — the behavior that produced six plans; a letter turns relapse into a reading event. Cost/placement: forty-five minutes, once; Phase 0, Day 4–5.

#### 4.1.3 Plan-debt accounting

Essence: your blueprint-to-artifact ratio does not start at zero — it opens in debt, roughly 370 KB of prior plans (~120 pages; the counter keeps the 370 figure for sentiment, not audit) as the opening balance, so shipping becomes repayment (dim03 C2). Mechanism: one scorecard line, `plan pages outstanding = 370 − (artifacts × 10)`; at ten pages of credit per ship, thirty-seven ships clears it (about eight months, matching the old plans' own Phase-3 horizons), and at zero the metric retires. The framing is amortization, not indictment: the plans were the tuition; shipping is the degree. Anti-trap: a sign-flip on an existing metric (the ≤3:1 ceiling) — zero new surface, psychology flipped from permissive to urgent; the flagged shame-spiral risk is guarded by the tuition framing itself. Cost/placement: five minutes; Phase 0, one footnote line in the scorecard.

### 4.2 Making the six-plan corpus pay rent

#### 4.2.1 Ship-the-Scorecard

Essence: your first public artifact is not an essay, demo, or kit but a tiny public page of the Core-3 numbers, updated weekly — accountability made visible (dim03 B1). Mechanism: one static page, four fields — `shipped: 1 · humans: 2 · streak: 12 days · updated <date>` — and updating it *is* the weekly ship for month one. Ship and measurement become the same object — the artifact cannot exist without the behavior, and non-shipping shows publicly as a stale date. A live scorecard answers the old open-source lesson ("只发目录，不发使用说明"): neither directory nor essay, but a heartbeat others can watch. Anti-trap: it removes the highest-threshold ship (polished content) exactly when the habit must form; the flagged performing-for-the-dashboard risk is mitigated because the numbers measure external behavior, never internal elegance. Cost/placement: one hour setup, five minutes weekly; Phase 0, Day 5 — it *is* the Day-5 ship. Host it anywhere you already have an account (a public note, a repo page, a pinned post). If none exists, week 1's ship defaults to sending the three numbers to your Day-0 witness — creating a new platform account is forbidden this month (do-NOT #2).

### 4.3 Borrowing weight from a human

#### 4.3.1 人类仲裁者 Human Arbiter

Essence: one real human as external arbiter with two narrow powers — countersigning amendments to `PLAN_LOCK.md`, and receiving the three-number scorecard monthly (dim03 F1). The corpus's deepest finding is that AI commitments have zero weight (资格不是被赋予的，是被重量换来的); no AI can deliver the "你这样下去不行" that comes only from someone who bears consequences (insight I5), so weight is borrowed from a human. Mechanism: the ask, verbatim — see Chapter 1's optional second line — and the Day-0 message recipient is the natural candidate. Fifteen minutes a month; the scorecard arrives as three numbers, no essay; their "no" costs them nothing and costs you a seven-day delay — that asymmetry is the entire device. Anti-trap: solitary re-planning becomes impossible, and contact is installed structurally around the smallest unit of accountability instead of performed for an audience. Flags: the wrong person is sycophancy with a human face — selection rule, someone who has said no to you before; the Arbiter is recommended, not blocking (CV #4) — if you are refused or no name exists, that fact is the scorecard's most important datum. Cost/placement: one awkward ten-minute ask; Phase 0, Day 0 — the Day-0 message doubles as the ask.

### 4.4 Social structure beyond the quota

#### 4.4.1 做一次别人的读者

Essence: for two weeks you play reader for one friend's raw material — daily five-minute contract responses on their fragments — before asking anyone to join your system (dim03 F3). All six prior plans routed contact one way — you ship outward, the other person is audience; this reverses the flow. Mechanism: the ask, verbatim — "两周，每天发我三行，我每天回你三条。" You run the 读者契约 by hand — the cabinet may assist within the design budget — three lines in, three replies out, fourteen days. Yield: the contract's failure modes learned from the inside — what gets read, what stings, what makes someone skip a day; a first external user of the protocol without you finishing anything; and a 共建者 candidate found by serving — the only anti-守门人 path, because service reverses the gatekeeper's power flow. Anti-trap: time-boxed, output-capped, and it *is* the human-contact quota for those weeks; if the friend lacks three lines a day, take a willing online acquaintance, and if none exists, postpone — it must never block Phases 0–1. Cost/placement: five minutes a day for fourteen days, plus one ask; Phase 2, weeks 3–4.

#### 4.4.2 撞见 quota

Essence: one countable weekly act in which your work or your person becomes bumpable-into by an independent will that did not plan the encounter (dim03 F4). Directed contact keeps control; undirected exposure surrenders it, and the gift no system gives is precisely 被另一个独立的意志意外地撞见. Mechanism: weekly, one act of ungated findability — a substantive three-sentence comment on a stranger's project, the scorecard link posted where you are unknown, or one live event where no one expects you — bound by the give-first rule: no pointing at your own work. Log it in `撞见/YYYY-WW.md` — what you left, where, what returned; usually nothing returns, and silence is data, while a reply is earned serendipity, logged apart from "contact." Anti-trap: one act, one log line — "wait to be discovered" becomes a weekly concrete throw with no audience management possible; drive-by self-promotion is blocked by the give-first rule. Cost/placement: 20–30 minutes weekly; Phase 2, weeks 3 onward.

### 4.5 Protected aliveness

#### 4.5.1 放空协议 Unrecorded Hour

Essence: one scheduled hour per week the system is forbidden from capturing — no log, no sensor, no retrospective entry — the constitutionally protected blind spot (dim03 F2). All six prior plans were total-capture architectures; the warrant for the exception is your own closing question — "这种放空到底是不是活着？" — asked as practice instead of left as accident. Mechanism: a weekly fixed slot and one rule — nothing from this hour enters the repo in any form: no diary line, no emotion tag, no "I rested" — with the scorecard checking only the binary self-attest that it happened. After eight weeks you have personal data on whether unrecorded hours feel more alive than recorded ones — the meaning-metric dissolved as practice rather than defined as number (insight I8). Anti-trap: pure deletion — any elaboration (a ritual, a template, a "rest log") is forbidden by the rule itself; the excuse-bucket risk is guarded by the scheduled slot and the attest. Cost/placement: zero; Phase 1, week 2 onward, permanent — and it should feel strange; that feeling is the point.

### 4.6 Capability options (Phase 2 flags, not builds)

#### 4.6.1 Absence detection, staged send, blind-dissent agents

These three are flags, not builds — standing options, each eligible only after its manual version has run. One rule governs all three: if the workspace can't do this in one afternoon, run the manual version and do not build infrastructure — use everything, build almost nothing (F5 §6).

Absence detection is the system that knocks first (dim03 D3). Every prior plan was pull-based — you had to show up for anything to work, so none governs a disappeared user. The mechanism is one scheduled job over the repo: no commit in 72 hours delivers the quiet-quit relapse letter unsolicited, through whatever channel the workspace can reach; no human contact in fourteen days delivers the third letter plus three staged human-facing options. Cap: one ping per trigger class per week, every ping logged — the relapse audit trail is itself data. Eligible once the letters exist.

Staged send is automation up to, never past, the button (dim03 D1). On day six of a week with an undone ship or contact, the agent prepares the send to the final click — post drafted, recipient filled — and notifies you: staged, one click remaining. Your veto is doing nothing; the staging auto-discards after 48 hours, logged as data. The button stays yours; only the friction around it — open the app, paste, format, address, exactly where 递出去 dies — is removed. The no-automation fallback (paste-ready message plus exact URL) delivers most of the value. Eligible after two fully manual loops, so the button's meaning is learned before it is made easy.

Blind-dissent agents make the cabinet real instead of theatrical (dim03 D2). 猎人, 怀疑者, and 审计员 run as three separate scheduled agents with disjoint memory, not personas in one session. 怀疑者 is forbidden from reading 猎人's output before writing its own — blind dissent, or it is commentary, not dissent — and its memory holds the veto log and red lines but not your latest enthusiasm. You read the collision at the weekly review — disagreement from a process that cannot see the performance, structurally closer to 拆台 than one model role-playing six voices. The fallback is the persona version, permanently; plumbing temptation is the named risk. Cost of each option: one afternoon, capped; Phase 2, each behind its stated gate.

### 4.7 The museum note

#### 4.7.1 What was rejected, and why

The honest deliverable includes the candidates that did not make the cut — filed now to `plan-seeds.md` and `museum/`, five lines maximum each, never a document, re-admissible only through the PLAN_LOCK amendment procedure (CV #3; dim03 G). Autopsy-as-artifact — publishing the six-plan autopsy as your first substantive artifact — is attractive because the material already exists, and rejected for the same reason: authored content exactly when the highest-threshold ship must be lowered, converting diagnosis into a performance of diagnosis. Sixth-worldline freeze — freezing the six plans' predictions and scoring which AI modeled you best — is rejected as another scoring ritual: measurement apparatus is planning surface; at zero execution you do not need to know which blueprint was most prophetic. Reader-for-hire — selling the 读者契约 as a paid reading service for strangers' corpora — is gated behind eight green weeks because premature pursuit is worldline A with revenue as the excuse. All architecture expansions are rejected as a class: the innovation axis of this chapter is behavioral, not architectural (insight I2), and anything that makes the machine more elaborate before it has run eight weeks is blueprint inflation by definition. If a rejected idea keeps pulling at you, that pull is data — write its five lines in `plan-seeds.md` and let the lock, the cooling period, and the Arbiter decide. That is what a last plan means.

## 6. The Operating Cadence

This chapter is the machine's clock. Everything in it is sized for a tired Tuesday: the daily loop costs ≤30 minutes with a 10-minute floor, the first 72 hours are scheduled to the minute, and every failure mode you are statistically likely to hit is already mapped to a response you pre-commit to now, while you are calm (dim04 §5). You do not negotiate with this chapter at midnight. You run it.

**Precondition — Day 0, the fuse, is covered in Chapter 1.** Within 24 hours of first reading this plan, you sent the content-free Day-0 message — 「我开始了。三十天后给你看第一个东西。不用回复——你只是我的见证。」 — to one named human, and you created `LifeOS/` by pasting Chapter 5's kit. If that did not happen, stop here and do Chapter 1. Nothing below works without the fuse, because the fuse is the only step that cannot wait for polish — it is content-free by design (CV #2). The 72 hours below therefore start with the kit already pasted and the recipient already named. Do not re-create the folder; do not "improve" the pasted files before Day 1. You begin by filling blanks, not by building.

### 6.2 Daily / weekly / monthly

The cadence has three gears (dim04 §4). The daily gear is the only one that runs every day, and it exists in two sizes because your energy is not constant: the 10-minute floor is the version that must survive your worst week, and the 30-minute full is the default for ordinary days. The weekly gear is the scoreboard and the correction. The monthly gear is the audit that catches what scores cannot: drift, sycophancy, and the system quietly replacing the life it was built to serve. Misses are handled by the downgrade table in §6.3 — never by doubling up, never by guilt.

#### 6.2.1 10-min floor and 30-min full daily checklists; Sunday weekly review ≤15 min; monthly mirror/archaeology/boundary/health

**DAILY — 10-min floor.** Three lines, one paste, one save. The floor exists so that "zero" — the only real failure — requires actively choosing silence (dim04 §4).

```
DAILY — 10-MIN FLOOR
[ ] 3-line entry: record / feeling [效价,唤醒度,掌控感] / tomorrow
[ ] Paste boot instruction + entry → chatbot → paste reply back
[ ] Save as diary/YYYY-MM-DD.md
```

**DAILY — 30-min full.** The floor plus the fields that make the reader useful over months: the anchor line (which future you diffed today), one sensor datum (so entries stay pinned to reality), and one real question. The tab harvest converts window-closing into capture instead of loss; the TASKS sweep keeps new ideas seeded rather than chased mid-task (dim04 §4).

```
DAILY — 30-MIN FULL
[ ] Floor (above), plus anchor line + one sensor datum + one question
[ ] Request the full 5-field AI delivery (adds branch + external suggestion)
[ ] Tab harvest: close ONE open tab/window by writing a SESSIONS.md card first
[ ] TASKS.md sweep: new ideas → seeds, never chased mid-task
[ ] Save/commit
```

**WEEKLY — Sunday, ≤15 min + 递出去.** Create a recurring Sunday calendar event and paste this checklist inside the event itself — forgetful-proof, no document to find (dim04 §6). You score the Core-3 scorecard — 3 numbers plus the level — because measurement must cost less than the work it measures; the harder red-line functions run as triggers in §6.3 instead of as weekly arithmetic (CV #1). The freeze is the 预测误差报告: the 3-line diff between what happened and what the future diary predicted, and it is the steering signal of the whole system.

The weekly-review checklist is pasted from §5.2.4 — keep only one operative copy (in the repo, from the kit).

**MONTHLY — first Sunday, +30 min appended to the weekly review.** Four checks, one added send. The mirror check catches a system that only ever tells you what you scripted; prompt archaeology diffs your own decision profile month over month; the boundary audit takes one delegated routine back by hand and checks whether you are falling asleep at the wheel; the health trio is forced-choice Y/N so it cannot be fudged into a paragraph (dim04 §4). ### 6.3 The downgrade decision table

This table makes it an execution: every trigger is an objective signal you can check in under a minute, and every response was committed now, not at the moment of failure, "so that decision is never again made by mood at midnight" (dim04 §5). It executes under LAW 12 without renegotiation; amending it requires a veto-log entry. When a row fires, you do the response — you do not first hold a meeting with yourself about whether you really feel like a person who is failing.

### 6.4 Execution without willpower + the do-NOT list

Willpower is not a component of this system. The design basis is the evidence about you in the source files: humans are "spontaneous, nondeterministic, error prone" with small working memory, and do their best work when highly focused, goal-driven, and interest-seeking (dim04 §6). So no recurring action is allowed to depend on remembering, deciding, or feeling motivated. Each gets three engineered supports: a **trigger** bolted to a behavior you already have, a **tiny version** a low-energy self can still complete, and a **recovery rule** with zero debt — a miss is a design case, not a moral failure (LAW 10).

#### 6.4.1 Trigger/tiny-version/recovery table per recurring action

| Recurring action | Trigger (time/place/event) | Tiny version | Recovery after a miss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily contract | The moment you open the chatbot anyway (interest-driven, so piggyback the existing habit); fallback: one 22:00 phone alarm whose text IS the 3-line template | 3 lines, floor format | Floor next day; never double; 3 misses → table row #2 |
| Emotion tags | Embedded in the contract format — the entry is incomplete without 3 numbers | Three digits | None needed; format enforces |
| SESSIONS.md card | Closing any window/lid — the card is the *price of closing* (sensors-not-tabs: capture, extract, close) | One card, 4 fields | Skip freely, no debt; cards are cheap by design |
| Tab harvest | The same window-close event | ONE tab, not ten | Skip; the backlog never accrues interest |
| Weekly review + 递出去 | Sunday calendar event with the checklist pasted inside the event itself (forgetful-proof: no document to find) | Scorecard numbers only (5 min) | Monday makeup; total miss = week defaults yellow; 递出去 miss → table row #6's 48h rule |
| Cabinet review | Item 5 of the weekly checklist | 怀疑者 only, on ONE entry | Skip; firebreak is the last thing to drop |
| Monthly mirror/archaeology/boundary | First Sunday, appended to weekly review | The three forced-choice Y/N health questions | Skip to next month; the Y/N trio is the floor |

(dim04 §6)

One structural note protects this cadence from 内卷卡死: the system deliberately alternates P-mode (execute the loop: daily contracts, weekly ships) and R-mode (reflect: the monthly archaeology and mirror check) instead of letting either run forever. P-mode forever is motion without consolidation; R-mode forever is reflection that never ships — the named failure of every plan that came before this one. The calendar above already contains both modes on schedule, so you never have to decide which mode you are in; the day of the week decides (dim04 §6).

#### 6.4.2 The 10-item do-NOT list for the first 30 days

Each item below feels like progress. That is exactly why it is forbidden: pseudo-productive behavior is your failure mode's native camouflage, and the first 30 days are when it will offer itself most persuasively (dim04 §7). These are not guidelines. Violating one is data for the veto log, and several map directly onto downgrade-table rows.

1. **Do not write or extend any architecture/blueprint document.** Any architecture paragraph must end in a ≤2-hour checkbox or go to `/museum` — "honored, kept, not acted on" (dim04 §7).
2. **Do not install, buy, or evaluate any new tool or platform.** A platform switch must promise +10 points on next week's forecast, in writing, or it is rejected as 扫描者陷阱 behavior (dim04 §7).
3. **Do not automate anything.** Manual first; week 3+ you may automate exactly ONE most-hated step, never more (dim04 §7).
4. **Do not start building the MVP / app / canvas / agent.** Building your own platform is forbidden until the manual loop has run for weeks — the loop must run on files and prompts before it earns automation (dim04 §7).
5. **Do not overwrite or "improve" any past entry.** Corrections append as new dated files (LAW 3) (dim04 §7).
6. **Do not reorganize, tag, or restructure the archive.** No taxonomy schemes. Maintenance replacing making is the named failure; 整理本身就是一种告别 (dim04 §7).
7. **Do not polish anything for an imaginary audience, and do not perform insight for the AI.** The blueprint becoming a performance for an imaginary audience is the diagnosed failure, not a style choice (dim04 §7).
8. **Do not let the AI whisper during human conversations, and do not delegate any message to a human.** Prepare before, debrief after, never during (dim04 §7).
9. **Do not treat the chatbot as the relationship ceiling or route acute emotional need to it.** Painkiller principle: useful in the moment, never confused with the cure (dim04 §7).
10. **Do not renegotiate the downgrade table mid-week, and do not wait for the 完美时机 to 递出去.** "他们只是不给自己留'再等等'这个选项。你每天给自己留了无数次" (dim04 §7).

That is the cadence. Day 1 starts tomorrow morning at the latest.

## 7. The Long Game — Do Not Touch Until Gates Pass

This chapter exists to be ignored until the gates pass. Reading further than this line before P0's gate is 蓝图引力 behavior.

### 7.1 What waits behind the gates

Everything below is listed so it stops colonizing the present. Each phase gets one deliberately boring paragraph, because the long game is a queue, not a vision. You do not prepare for these phases now; you pass a gate and the paragraph activates (dim02 §3.2).

#### 7.1.1 P2–P4, and he4o

**P2 · Nerves & Topology (weeks 3–8).** You adopt an existing branching canvas — you do not build one — and run the pure-git typed-edge convention underneath it: `forks_from`, `echoes`, `fulfills_future` first, six types later. Once a week you hold the merge ritual: the system proposes reconciliations between branches and `main`, you rule on every conflict, and every refusal lands in the veto-log, because 否决日志即人格边界. A one-script emotion-tag retrieval prototype and a weekly counterfactual review run alongside; no vector database, no infrastructure. **Gate: ≥1 branch cherry-picked into `main` + one genuinely surprising retrieval + a written MVP go/no-go memo.** (dim02 §3.2)

**P3 · Cabinet & First Strangers (weeks 6–10).** The cabinet becomes v1: at most three roles, scheduled — Hunter plus Editor plus Auditor, or Hunter plus Skeptic — and it must complete tasks you did not initiate, one worth keeping. If the P2 memo said go, you build the Reversible Pages MVP inside a six-week timebox, feature-frozen at week 8; if it said no, the evidence-backed no-build memo is itself a pass. Either way the phase ends in front of strangers, not in front of the mirror. **Gate: 5 non-friend users OR a public demo OR an evidence-backed no-build memo AND the cabinet completed tasks you did not initiate, one worth keeping.** (dim02 §3.2)

**P4 · Hive (month 3+).** You package the working system as public Kit v1 — reader contract, future diary, merge protocol — and you ship its 使用说明, not just its 目录, with a fork-acceptance pre-commitment written before anyone forks. The 蜂巢权限旋钮 may rise one notch, on branches only; `main` and external actions stay yours. The economy rail gets one x402-class spike: one evening, proof not revenue. Product design here is a report on a working system, not a blueprint — never before. **Gate: 60+ loop-days before any of it.** (dim02 §3.2; F5 §6)

**he4o.** Parallel, not merged. You study its cadence — small, daily, public, honestly-percentaged — and maybe you correspond, once there is something running to correspond about. You do not join it, do not fork it, do not plan toward it. Five of the six files felt the need to fence it off, which is itself evidence of pull. **Gate: none — it is a second lifetime project.** (dim02 §6 D7)

#### 7.1.2 The one metric

The long game has exactly one metric, and it is not users, revenue, or model quality: whether anything you set in motion keeps moving when you stop pushing (F4 §5.3). A friend pulled into co-building who still ships when you go quiet. A Kit strangers actually load without you in the room. A genome of memory that outlives any model you currently pay for. The leading indicators are already scheduled, and both are human: 做一次别人的读者, because a contract you can run for someone else no longer needs you as its product, and 撞见, because work that can be bumped into by an unplanned will has begun to exist outside your head. Reader-for-hire stays where it belongs — a plan-seed, gated at 8 green weeks, never a to-do in this chapter (dim03 E1).
